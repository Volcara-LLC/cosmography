# ADR 0016: Answer-Blinded Kerr Mapping Review and Receipt-Bound Comparison Preparation

**Status:** Accepted for package-only comparison-review preparation foundation | **Date:** 2026-07-19

## Context

ADR 0015 ends with one immutable, process-local admission that binds an injected-verifier-accepted runner receipt to exact raw bytes, exact answer-blinded normalized bytes, and one full comparison plan. That admission deliberately cannot enter the legacy comparison path. The legacy candidate describes caller-declared or synthetic execution, expects a different normalized artifact, and cannot truthfully carry receipt, review, or one-use capability provenance.

The next boundary must let a mapping specialist review how one frozen normalizer translates external output into Cosmography conventions without showing the specialist Cosmography reference outcomes, provisional tolerances, or comparison results. After that review, the package may reconstruct a candidate inside the full-plan boundary, but it still must not claim that the package authenticated the reviewer or runner, proved mapping correctness, performed a comparison, established independence, passed scientific validation, or gained render or release authority.

## Decision

- Preparation begins only with the exact process-issued admission, the exact admitted verified-reference bundle object, the parser-owned frozen plan, and the exact raw and answer-blinded normalized bytes retained by the admission context. Structural copies, another bundle capability, another plan, changed bytes, or a legacy candidate cannot cross the boundary.
- The mapping-review subject is structurally answer-blinded. It binds the admission, receipt, plan identity and scope, complete frozen mapping declaration, canonical mapping bytes, receipt-declared normalizer source digest, exact output descriptors, row count, and an opaque ordered association digest. It includes the receipt-declared runner and prior selection-review identities so the reviewer and verifier can enforce separation. Typed fields exclude reference case, request, report, and ray identifiers and checksums; reference outcomes, tolerances, and comparison results; and every downstream authority. The mapping declaration contains caller-controlled free text, so the subject explicitly records that the package has not verified that text is free of reference data; absence is a signed reviewer declaration checked only by the injected verifier. The opaque association digest commits the later package-side row join without disclosing its reference records.
- Subject identity and checksum are replayed independently before signing. Canonical subject bytes are generated from the strict parser-owned representation, and the subject binds the original execution-claim expiry. A mapping decision signs the exact canonical subject digest, not merely a caller-carried subject ID or checksum. Its all-true semantic assertions cover deterministic complete translation, no omission/duplication/reordering or answer-dependent adjustment, units and coordinates, momentum and conserved-quantity meanings, screen orientation, termination and event surfaces, azimuth representation, endpoint time and affine parameter, unresolved outcomes, scope-specific input/output preservation, answer blindness, source freezing, and complete transformation disclosure.
- Mapping review is a fourth role, separate from scientific-independence, security/execution, and license/output-rights selection review. The mapping reviewer ID, role grant, and key must be distinct from the receipt-declared runner identity, grant, and key and from all three prior selection-review identities, grants, and keys. Review time must follow subject freezing, and review and verification must remain strictly within both the signed decision expiry and the original execution-claim expiry.
- The signed decision remains a declaration awaiting an injected verifier. Hashing an opaque signature envelope does not verify a signature, authenticate a key, or grant authority. The injected verifier receives frozen subject and decision values plus a detached copy of the exact signature-envelope bytes and must return one strict receipt binding the exact subject, decision, statement, envelope, reviewer, grant, key, policy, and check time. It must assert signature and purpose verification, current role/key validity and revocation, exact admission/mapping/normalizer/output/row bindings, evidence and semantic-assertion review, answer blindness, identity separation, and non-synthetic/non-caller-declared review. The package independently recomputes all identity-separation predicates. The verifier remains explicitly unauthenticated by this package.
- Caller-owned raw, normalized, and mapping-signature byte arrays are copied and bounded before structured evidence is traversed. Subject, decision, chronology, identity, plan, bundle, and exact-byte checks finish synchronously. The admission is then burned immediately before the first asynchronous verifier call. A verifier rejection, exception, malformed or mismatched receipt, concurrent call, or later reconstruction failure cannot be retried with changed evidence.
- Candidate reconstruction occurs only after verifier acceptance and only from parser-owned process context. Rows are joined in exact plan order. Ordinals, pixels, source blinded-row identity/checksum, and admitted plan metadata must match exactly and cannot be missing, duplicated, or reordered. Every numeric value—screen coordinates, normalized initial momentum, constants, termination, and endpoint—comes exclusively from the receipt-bound runner-normalized row. The package never substitutes a reference numeric value. Only plan case/request/report/ray identities and checksums are attached by ordinal; no reference outcome or tolerance is copied.
- The reconstructed candidate uses a new receipt-bound private schema and new identity/checksum scopes. It is deliberately incompatible with the legacy caller-declared candidate and comparator. Its canonical bytes are package-derived, capped separately at 3 MiB so the frozen 625-row plan ceiling remains representable, and explicitly distinct from the admitted answer-blinded normalized bytes. The complete candidate, exact raw and normalized bytes, exact mapping signature envelope, plan, bundle, subject, decision, and verifier receipt remain in a private `WeakMap` keyed by the issued preparation capability.
- The public preparation record exposes only evidence bindings, row-association summaries, a candidate canonical-byte binding, and narrow process facts. Structural copies are rejected. `mappingReviewDecisionAcceptedByInjectedBoundary`, exact-byte replay, candidate reconstruction, and capability issuance may be true; `injectedMappingVerifierAuthorityAuthenticatedByPackage`, `mappingCorrectnessVerified`, `executionVerified`, `candidateAdmissionAllowed`, `comparisonAllowed`, `comparisonExecuted`, scientific and independent validation, rendering, production use, placeholder replacement, release eligibility, publication, and activation remain false. `independentReference` remains `not-run`.
- This package still performs no filesystem or network I/O, source acquisition, archive extraction, build, shell, process, worker, native loading, sandbox operation, signing, output capture, external execution, or real comparison. Tests use synthetic in-memory capabilities, declarations, receipts, signatures, and bytes only. No real external implementation, reviewer, runner, mapping review, output, prepared candidate, or comparison exists.

## Consequences

The package now has a fail-closed transition from one admitted answer-blinded output to one private, full-plan candidate prepared for a later comparison-review consumer. The review sees enough frozen mapping and identity evidence to make a scoped semantic decision without receiving the reference answers, while the package can attach private plan associations only after that decision is verified. Exact byte separation prevents the runner-normalized artifact from being relabeled as a package-derived candidate.

This remains process-local protocol evidence, not durable governance or scientific validation. A production system still needs an authenticated reviewer/key registry, authoritative time and revocation service, durable signed ledger, immutable evidence retention, cross-process atomic consumption, reproducible isolated runner, and verified source-to-executable and normalizer lineage. A later one-use consumer must compare the private candidate, preserve disagreement, and issue only a non-authoritative comparison record. Independent scientific adjudication, GPU/WebGL parity, emission and radiative transfer, image generation, named-device performance, accessibility, and release review remain separate gates. Nothing here completes BH1, a roadmap stage, or the product, and the visible procedural Black-hole Lab remains unchanged.

## Validation

Acceptance requires strict tests for deterministic answer-blinded subjects and canonical bytes; subject and signed-decision self-integrity; exact admission, bundle, plan, mapping, normalizer, output, row, statement, and signature-envelope binding; reviewer/runner/selection identity separation; decision and claim expiry; verifier receipt strictness; byte ceilings and hostile typed arrays; subject, decision, and capability substitution; pre-await detachment; one-use replay, concurrency, and burn-on-rejection or exception; exact row coverage and runner-numeric preservation; private candidate/public summary separation; structural-copy rejection; deep freezing; static no-I/O imports; and literal-false comparison, science, render, release, publication, and activation authority.

Those tests validate only the package contract. They cannot validate a real source repository, license, reviewer, signature, key registry, build, runner, sandbox, attestation, output, mapping, independent implementation, numerical agreement, or scientific conclusion.

The accepted checkpoint passes the complete relativity package check with 103 tests. Its focused receipt/admission/mapping-preparation suite passes all 23 cases, including six new preparation cases. TypeScript compilation and the static no-I/O import audit also pass.
