# ADR 0010: Fail-Closed Mars and Moon Surface Provenance

**Status:** Accepted for surface provenance foundation | **Date:** 2026-07-19

## Context

The Mars/Jezero and Moon/Apollo 17 experiences already separate observed-product descriptions, scientific reconstruction, interpolation, and immersive display. Their viewports are procedural and their source registers link to official archive, cartographic, localization, mission-media, and historical records. Those links and typed descriptions are useful acquisition plans, but they are not exact product manifests, evidence that bytes were retained intact, asset-level rights decisions, or physical camera poses.

Planetary surface products have failure modes that a generic source URL cannot represent. Orthomosaics and elevation models must preserve body-specific coordinate systems, longitude direction/domain, projection, horizontal and vertical datums, footprint, resolution versus positional accuracy, seams, no-data values, gap filling, and elevation scale. Recorded-media products must preserve exact frame inventories, capture-time precision, camera/instrument state, color treatment, stitch lineage, and missing coverage. Terrain, localization, calibration, and media may all be individually valid while still lacking a validated transform into one renderable camera context.

Mars and the Moon need one reusable admission boundary rather than mission-specific boolean readiness flags. The boundary must support offline contract work without implying that any source was fetched, any product byte was cleared, or any capture pose was solved.

## Decision

- Each surface experience has a strict pre-ingest ledger of reference-only product requirements and withheld capture contexts. A requirement binds its local opaque ID, experience/body, product kind, intended evidence class, authority and source release, credential-free HTTPS record, exact-record or selection-pending identity, source/evidence/capture references, checked time, intended use, and concrete blockers. Reference records permit metadata display and external links only; `assetBytesPresent`, `renderAllowed`, and `releaseEligible` are always false.
- The current Jezero ledger declares requirements for an exact orbital-context product, distinct CTX and HiRISE image/elevation products, confidence, local mesh, PLACES localization, Mastcam-Z calibration, and three panorama records. The current Apollo 17 ledger declares an exact orbital-context product, distinct LROC orthomosaic and DEM requirements, traverse and station localization, Hasselblad calibration, and three landing/Station 5/Station 6 panorama records. These are record identities and selection targets, not admitted artifacts.
- A future frozen product uses a separate strict reviewed-manifest state. It requires an immutable exact product identity; at least primary-data and exact source-label assets; extraction-safe filenames and credential-free, query-free HTTPS source URLs; byte lengths, SHA-256 digests, unique canonical `surface/` object keys, capture times, exact asset-target rights statements, and complete source-original or derivative lineage. It also binds AI involvement, representation, intended use, domain-specific metadata, processing lineage, a retained passed validation report, canonicalization, and a canonical manifest digest.
- Cartographic metadata cannot inherit Earth WGS/EPSG/ITRF/NAD frames and must bind the manifest body, planetary frame, projection, datum, vertical reference when applicable, coordinate convention, valid extent, resolution units, accuracy, no-data/gap behavior, seams, and one-times default elevation. Its projection digest must match one retained projection-definition asset. Recorded media retains unique source-frame IDs, explicit time precision, instrument, dimensions/color/stitch disclosure, and a placement-withheld state. Geometry products bind their frame chain to the manifest body and retain units, validity time, coverage, and position/orientation uncertainty.
- A reviewed manifest remains serialized non-authority with `runtimeUseAllowed: false`, `capturePlacementAllowed: false`, and `releaseEligible: false`. Before verification, a separately authorized in-process reviewed-selection token binds the reference requirement to the exact selected identity, source descriptor, representation class, and intended use; structural copies fail. Runtime verification requires an explicit evaluation time, recomputes the canonical digest, rechecks current intended-use rights, and demands exact parsed receipts for every retained asset and validation-report artifact. The parsed passed report must reproduce the full body/source/product context, assets, rights, metadata, processing, representation, and required checks; a boolean result is insufficient. Only that process can issue a second non-serializable runtime token. The token permits product runtime use only and continues to deny capture placement and release eligibility.
- Surface capture placement is an independent future gate. The current schema intentionally admits only `withheld-incomplete-surface-capture`. Each context preserves reported-time precision, an approximate display anchor or unresolved coordinate, required product requirements, source references, and blockers. Media, localization, calibration, optical-center, orientation, terrain-frame, uncertainty, render, and release authority all remain false. There is no parseable renderable surface-pose branch and no procedural camera frustum fallback.
- The Mars and Moon pages expose ledger counts and blockers as a readiness panel. They identify their coordinates as region/display anchors, describe stations as reference contracts, and render no camera frustum. The UI cannot turn a reference or raw reviewed manifest into runtime or placement authority.

## Consequences

Reference research, source selection, and future acquisition requirements can advance in Git without downloading large surface assets or overstating readiness. Mars and Moon use the same contracts, so a new body or mission pack can reuse the boundary instead of adding permissive local fields.

Exact product acquisition, quarantine receipts, asset-level rights review, frozen manifests, selection review, validation reports, parsed artifact receipts, runtime-token issuance, terrain rendering, physical camera-pose solving, capture-placement validation, release governance, activation, and deployment remain separate work. At this checkpoint, zero Mars or Moon surface bytes are admitted, zero surface selection/runtime tokens exist, and all six current capture contexts remain withheld. This decision does not complete Stage 4, Stage 6, or the product.

Changing the foundation atlas truth labels changes its canonical release bundle. The embedded fixture content digest, manifest digest, and seeded control-plane manifest must advance together; that bookkeeping still does not sign or activate a release.

## Validation

Scientific-model tests cover reference-only byte/authority laundering; immutable product identity; independently authorized reviewed-selection capabilities; deterministic manifest hashing; exact parsed asset/report receipts and structural-token boundaries; passed validation after rights review; exact, current, sufficient rights; query-free URLs, extraction-safe filenames, media extensions, and object keys; source-original primary/label assets; retained projection definitions; exact validation-input binding; unique identities; causal chronology; body-specific CRS, longitude, extent, no-data, accuracy, and elevation rules; generative-output disclosure; derivative parent/cycle checks; frame inventory and time precision; withheld capture flags; and duplicate, dangling, non-reciprocal, cross-body, cross-experience, or stale pre-ingest records.

The web integration parses both concrete ledgers at module load, binds their source/evidence/capture references, exposes zero admitted artifacts and tokens, uses region-anchor/pose-withheld language, and omits a camera-frustum element. Its acceptance tests must keep those visible fail-closed disclosures from regressing as the interface evolves.
